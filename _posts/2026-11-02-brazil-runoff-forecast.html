Method: I calibrate the strategy on the 2022 runoff and apply it to the 2026 runoff, with polling stations entering in the actual order of the count.

Context: Brazil elects its president in two rounds. Candidates are identified by ballot number; the two runoff finalists in both elections are numbers 13 and 22, and I refer to them simply as 13 and 22. A polling station (seção eleitoral) is the unit served by one voting machine. States appear by their two-letter codes (SP for São Paulo, for example). I use data from Brazil’s Superior Electoral Court (TSE). All times are Brasília time.

1. Getting the data

1.1 Sources

I use TSE open data for 2022 and 2026 and check the SHA-256 hash of each file on download.

Key File Content Use
votes_{year} votacao_secao_{year}_BR.zip Votes by polling station, candidate and round (includes voters abroad, state code ZZ) Votes for each candidate
detail_{year} detalhe_votacao_secao_{year}.zip Registered voters, turnout, abstentions, blank and null votes, and timestamps by station Counting order and station profiles
history_2022 Historico_Totalizacao_Presidente_BR_2T_2022.zip 2022 runoff tabulations, release by release Cross-check only; not used in the models

1.2 Raw data

  • CSV files inside ZIP archives, separated by ; and encoded in latin1, with one row per station × office × round × ballot option.
  • Station key: SG_UF, CD_MUNICIPIO (the TSE code, not the IBGE code), NR_ZONA and NR_SECAO.
  • Filters: CD_CARGO == 1 (president) and NR_VOTAVEL < 90, which keeps only votes cast for candidates.
  • Timestamps, in Brasília time: DT_RECEBIMENTO_BU_HOR_TSE (the station’s tally report reaches the TSE) and DT_PRIM_TOT_PARCIAL_HOR_TSE (the station enters the tabulation).
  • I order the count by tabulation time. The receipt time is never later (the median gap is 36 s in 2022 and 24 s in 2026) and I do not use it.

1.3 Prepared dataset

data_en/tse_{year}.csv.gz, with one row per polling station:

Column Meaning
state, municipality, zone, station Station key
received, tabulated Receipt time and first tabulation time
a1, v1 Votes for 13 and valid votes for all candidates, first round
a2, b2 Votes for 13 and for 22, runoff

  • In 2026, I discard the votes for candidate 28, which the TSE classified as annulled.

Computes the SHA-256 hash of a file to verify its integrity.

def sha256(path, block=1 << 20):
    h = hashlib.sha256()
    with open(path, 'rb') as f:
        while b := f.read(block):
            h.update(b)
    return h.hexdigest()

Downloads a TSE file if it is not already present and verifies its SHA-256 hash.

def download(key):
    url, expected = SOURCES[key]
    target_path = RAW / url.rsplit('/', 1)[1]
    if not target_path.exists():
        print('downloading', url, flush=True)
        partial = target_path.with_suffix('.part')
        with urllib.request.urlopen(url) as r, open(partial, 'wb') as f:
            shutil.copyfileobj(r, f)
        partial.rename(target_path)
    actual = sha256(target_path)
    if actual != expected:
        raise ValueError(f'SHA-256 mismatch in {target_path.name}: {actual} != {expected}')
    return target_path

def read_zip(archive, columns):
    with ZipFile(archive) as z:
        name, = [n for n in z.namelist() if n.endswith('_BR.csv')]
        with z.open(name) as csv:
            yield from pd.read_csv(csv, sep=';', encoding='latin1', chunksize=200_000,

Converts the votes and detail ZIP files into one row per polling station and checks the official totals.

def prepare(year, votes, detail, output):
    """Two national TSE ZIP files -> one row per polling station, with votes for 13 and 22 and timestamps."""
    chunks = []
    for p in read_zip(votes, ['NR_VOTAVEL', 'QT_VOTOS']):
        p = p.loc[(p.CD_CARGO == 1) & (p.NR_VOTAVEL < 90)].copy()
        if year == 2026:                      # candidate 28: votes classified as annulled
            p = p[p.NR_VOTAVEL != 28]
        t1 = p.NR_TURNO == 1
        p['v1'] = p.QT_VOTOS.where(t1, 0)
        p['a1'] = p.QT_VOTOS.where(t1 & (p.NR_VOTAVEL == 13), 0)
        p['a2'] = p.QT_VOTOS.where(~t1 & (p.NR_VOTAVEL == 13), 0)
        p['b2'] = p.QT_VOTOS.where(~t1 & (p.NR_VOTAVEL == 22), 0)
        chunks.append(p.groupby(KEY_COLS)[['a1', 'v1', 'a2', 'b2']].sum())
    counts = pd.concat(chunks).groupby(level=list(range(4))).sum()

    time_cols = ['DT_RECEBIMENTO_BU_HOR_TSE', 'DT_PRIM_TOT_PARCIAL_HOR_TSE']
    stations = pd.concat([p.loc[(p.NR_TURNO == 2) & (p.CD_CARGO == 1), KEY_COLS + time_cols]
                        for p in read_zip(detail, time_cols)]).set_index(KEY_COLS)
    assert stations.index.is_unique and counts.index.difference(stations.index).empty

    base = stations.join(counts).sort_index()
    for k in counts:
        base[k] = base[k].fillna(0).astype('int32')
    for k in time_cols:
        base[k] = pd.to_datetime(base[k], format='%d/%m/%Y %H:%M:%S', errors='coerce')
    base = base.reset_index().rename(columns=dict(zip(
        KEY_COLS + time_cols, ['state', 'municipality', 'zone', 'station', 'received', 'tabulated'])))

    measured = (len(base), int(base.v1.sum()), int(base.a1.sum()), int(base.a2.sum()), int(base.b2.sum()))
    assert measured == CONTROL[year], (measured, CONTROL[year])
    assert base.tabulated.notna().all()
    base.to_csv(output, index=False, compression={'method': 'gzip', 'compresslevel': 3, 'mtime': 0})
    print(year, measured, 'without a receipt timestamp:', base.received.isna().sum(), flush=True)

2. Modeling

I tested five approaches: 1. National swing: measures how much the vote distribution shifted between the two rounds in the stations already counted. 2. Logit swing: the same idea on the logit scale, which prevents shares below 0% or above 100%. 3. Ridge: learns the change between rounds as a function of the first-round share and station size. It tries to correct the bias from early stations being small or atypical. 4. Gradient boosting: uses the same variables as Ridge but allows nonlinear relationships. 5. Hierarchical Bayesian logit: groups stations into bands by the first-round share of 13 and by size. Each band has its own swing, drawn from a common distribution learned from the stations already counted.

2.1 Simulation and definitions

I simulate the 2022 count: stations enter in tabulated order and, at each checkpoint, every model is refit from scratch using only the stations counted so far and projects the national result.

  • Target quantity: the share of 13 in runoff valid votes, summed over all stations. Above 50%, 13 wins.
  • Votes counted: runoff valid votes (a2 + b2) in the stations counted, as a share of the official total, which is known only when the count ends.
  • Checkpoints: 0.25%, 0.5%, 1%, 2.5%, 5%, 10% and 20% of the votes. Each station enters whole, along with all stations sharing its timestamp, so the realized share can slightly exceed the nominal one.
  • Inputs to each model: both rounds for the stations counted; for the remaining stations, only the first-round share of 13 (p1 = a1 / v1) and size (v1). I do not use state or municipality.
  • Projection: (A + Σ w·q) / (V + Σ w). A and V are the votes for 13 and the valid votes counted so far; q is the predicted share in each remaining station; w = k · v1 is its estimated vote count, with k = V / Σ v1 over the counted stations.

2.2 How each model projects

# Model Projected share in the remaining stations
0 Running tally (baseline) No projection: uses the share counted so far, A / V
1 National swing p1 plus a constant, calibrated to reproduce the result of the counted stations
2 Logit swing Same as above, on the logit scale
3 Ridge p1 plus the change predicted from p1, p1², log v1 and p1·log v1, weighted by runoff votes
4 Gradient boosting p1 plus the change predicted from p1 and log v1, also weighted
5 Hierarchical Bayes Logit swing across 32 bands (8 of p1 × 4 of size), with partial pooling and a credible interval

Model 5. I estimate each band’s swing from the counted stations and shrink it toward the overall mean when the band has few votes (random effects, DerSimonian-Laird). Bands with no counted stations inherit the overall mean. The result is a distribution for the final share of 13, which yields the median and the 95% interval. The interval does not account for uncertainty in the number of votes still to come, or for differences between counted and uncounted stations within a band.

def build(df):
    """Per-station arrays, strata and counting order for each clock."""
    a1, v1 = df.a1.to_numpy(float), df.v1.to_numpy(float)
    a2, b2 = df.a2.to_numpy(float), df.b2.to_numpy(float)
    v2 = a2 + b2
    p1 = np.divide(a1, v1, out=np.full(len(df), a1.sum() / v1.sum()), where=v1 > 0)
    p1 = np.clip(p1, EPS, 1 - EPS)
    p2 = np.divide(a2, v2, out=np.zeros(len(df)), where=v2 > 0)
    band_p = np.digitize(p1, np.quantile(p1, np.linspace(0, 1, 9)[1:-1]))
    band_v = np.digitize(v1, np.quantile(v1, [.25, .5, .75]))
    for r in CLOCKS:
        assert df[r].notna().all(), r
    order = {r: np.argsort(df[r].to_numpy(), kind='stable') for r in CLOCKS}
    return dict(a1=a1, v1=v1, a2=a2, v2=v2, p1=p1, p2=p2, stratum=band_p * 4 + band_v,
                order=order, times={r: df[r].to_numpy()[o] for r, o in order.items()},

Computes how many stations are counted at each checkpoint, without splitting stations that share a timestamp.

def batches(E, clock, checkpoints=CHECKPOINTS):
    """For each checkpoint, the polling stations counted (n): all those up to the timestamp that reaches it."""
    order, t = E['order'][clock], E['times'][clock]
    cumulative = np.cumsum(E['v2'][order]) / E['v2'].sum()
    output = []
    for checkpoint in checkpoints:
        k = np.searchsorted(cumulative, checkpoint)                    # first station that reaches the checkpoint
        output.append((checkpoint, int(np.searchsorted(t, t[k], side='right'))))   # complete the batch with the same timestamp
    return output

Separates counted and uncounted stations and estimates the votes in the remaining ones.

def context(E, order, n):
    """Counted (c) and uncounted (u) stations, counted totals and the votes expected in the remainder."""
    c, u = order[:n], order[n:]
    V, A = E['v2'][c].sum(), E['a2'][c].sum()
    return dict(c=c, u=u, V=V, A=A, wu=(V / E['v1'][c].sum()) * E['v1'][u])

Adds the counted and projected votes to obtain the national share of 13.

def combine(ctx, q):
    """National share of 13 = counted + projected over the remaining stations."""
    return (ctx['A'] + (ctx['wu'] * q).sum()) / (ctx['V'] + ctx['wu'].sum())

Model 1: adds a single change, measured in the counted stations, to the first-round share.

def national_swing(E, ctx):
    c, u = ctx['c'], ctx['u']
    w = E['v2'][c]
    delta = (ctx['A'] - (w * E['p1'][c]).sum()) / w.sum()      # reproduces the result of the counted stations
    return dict(estimate=combine(ctx, np.clip(E['p1'][u] + delta, 0, 1)))

Model 2: applies the same idea on the logit scale.

def logit_swing(E, ctx):
    c, u = ctx['c'], ctx['u']
    w, zc = E['v2'][c], logit(E['p1'][c])
    d = brentq(lambda d: (w * expit(zc + d)).sum() - ctx['A'], -6, 6)
    return dict(estimate=combine(ctx, expit(logit(E['p1'][u]) + d)))

Builds the Ridge and boosting features from p1 and size.

def features(E, idx):
    p, s = E['p1'][idx], np.log1p(E['v1'][idx])
    return np.column_stack([p, p ** 2, s, p * s])

Selects the counted stations with votes and computes their weights.

def vote_weights(E, c):
    """Counted stations with votes, and weights with mean 1 (runoff valid votes)."""
    w = E['v2'][c]
    ok = w > 0
    return ok, w[ok] / w[ok].mean()

Model 3: predicts the change between rounds with Ridge regression.

def ridge(E, ctx):
    c, u = ctx['c'], ctx['u']
    ok, w = vote_weights(E, c)
    m = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), RidgeCV(alphas=np.logspace(-3, 3, 13)))
    m.fit(features(E, c)[ok], (E['p2'][c] - E['p1'][c])[ok], ridgecv__sample_weight=w)
    return dict(estimate=combine(ctx, np.clip(E['p1'][u] + m.predict(features(E, u)), 0, 1)))

Model 4: predicts the change between rounds with gradient boosting.

def boosting(E, ctx):
    c, u = ctx['c'], ctx['u']
    ok, w = vote_weights(E, c)
    X = lambda idx: features(E, idx)[:, [0, 2]]               # p1 and log(v1)
    m = HistGradientBoostingRegressor(max_depth=3, max_iter=150, learning_rate=0.05,
                                      min_samples_leaf=50, random_state=0)
    m.fit(X(c)[ok], (E['p2'][c] - E['p1'][c])[ok], sample_weight=w)
    return dict(estimate=combine(ctx, np.clip(E['p1'][u] + m.predict(X(u)), 0, 1)))

Defines the logit swing values evaluated by the Bayesian model.

SWING_GRID = np.linspace(-1.5, 1.5, 61)                              # logit swings evaluated

Computes, for each band, the votes for 13 expected at each swing value.

def sum_by_stratum(E, idx, weight):
    """S[e, g] = sum of weight * expit(logit(p1) + SWING_GRID[g]) over the idx stations in stratum e."""
    z, est = logit(E['p1'][idx]), E['stratum'][idx]
    S = np.zeros((N_STRATA, SWING_GRID.size))
    for e in range(N_STRATA):
        m = est == e
        if m.any():
            S[e] = (weight[m, None] * expit(z[m, None] + SWING_GRID)).sum(0)
    return S

Model 5: estimates the swing per band and simulates the distribution of the final result.

def hierarchical_bayes(E, ctx, n_sim=4000, seed=0):
    c, u = ctx['c'], ctx['u']
    wc, est = E['v2'][c], E['stratum'][c]
    Ve = np.bincount(est, weights=wc, minlength=N_STRATA)
    Ae = np.bincount(est, weights=E['a2'][c], minlength=N_STRATA)
    ok = Ve > 0
    P = np.where(ok, (Ae + .5) / (Ve + 1), .5)                  # smoothed share (Jeffreys)
    Sc = sum_by_stratum(E, c, wc)
    # observed swing per stratum: the one that reproduces P in the counted stations of the stratum
    d_obs = np.array([np.interp(P[e] * Ve[e], Sc[e], SWING_GRID) if ok[e] else 0. for e in range(N_STRATA)])
    var_obs = np.where(ok, 1 / np.maximum(Ve * P * (1 - P), 1e-12), np.inf)

    # random effects: overall mean mu and between-strata variance tau2 (DerSimonian-Laird)
    w, d, k = 1 / var_obs[ok], d_obs[ok], ok.sum()
    mu_fe = (w * d).sum() / w.sum()
    Q = (w * (d - mu_fe) ** 2).sum()
    tau2 = (Q - (k - 1)) / (w.sum() - (w ** 2).sum() / w.sum()) if k > 1 else .05 ** 2
    tau2 = max(tau2, 1e-6)
    ws = 1 / (var_obs[ok] + tau2)
    mu, var_mu = (ws * d).sum() / ws.sum(), 1 / ws.sum()

    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    mu_s = rng.normal(mu, np.sqrt(var_mu), n_sim)
    Su = sum_by_stratum(E, u, ctx['wu'])
    projected = np.zeros(n_sim)
    for e in range(N_STRATA):
        if ok[e]:
            prec = 1 / var_obs[e] + 1 / tau2
            d_e = rng.normal((d_obs[e] / var_obs[e] + mu_s / tau2) / prec, prec ** -.5)
        else:
            d_e = rng.normal(mu_s, np.sqrt(tau2))
        projected += np.interp(d_e, SWING_GRID, Su[e])
    dist = (ctx['A'] + projected) / (ctx['V'] + ctx['wu'].sum())
    lo, med, hi = np.percentile(dist, [2.5, 50, 97.5])
    return dict(estimate=med, lo=lo, hi=hi)

def evaluate(E, clocks=CLOCKS, checkpoints=CHECKPOINTS):
    """Run every model at every checkpoint and clock; error in percentage points."""
    rows = []
    for r in clocks:
        for checkpoint, n in batches(E, r, checkpoints):
            ctx = context(E, E['order'][r], n)
            for name, model in MODELS.items():
                t0 = time.perf_counter()
                s = model(E, ctx)
                rows.append(dict(
                    clock=r, checkpoint=checkpoint, stations_pct=n / E['n'], votes_pct=ctx['V'] / E['v2'].sum(),
                    model=name, estimate=s['estimate'], lo=s.get('lo', np.nan), hi=s.get('hi', np.nan),
                    error_pp=100 * (s['estimate'] - E['final']),
                    winner_correct=(s['estimate'] > .5) == (E['final'] > .5),
                    seconds=time.perf_counter() - t0))
    out = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    out['abs_error_pp'] = out.error_pp.abs()
    return out

3. How many votes are needed

At what share of votes counted does the projection stay within 1, 0.5, 0.25 and 0.1 pp? I use the 2022 answer to set the model and the stopping point for 2026.

3.1 Definitions

  • Checkpoint grid: about 70 checkpoints on a log scale, from 0.1% to 50% of the votes, under the rules of section 2. I do not check the error between two checkpoints.
  • First crossing: the first checkpoint with an error below the limit.
  • Sustained crossing: the checkpoint from which the error stays below the limit at every later checkpoint, up to 50%. I use this as the criterion for the stopping rule, because an isolated crossing can be luck.

Finds the first and the sustained crossing below each limit.

def thresholds(res, E, clock='tabulated', limits=LIMITS):
    """First and sustained crossing below each limit, per model."""
    rows = []
    for model, g in res[res.clock == clock].groupby('model'):
        g = g.sort_values('votes_pct').reset_index(drop=True)
        for lim in limits:
            below = (g.abs_error_pp < lim).to_numpy()
            sustained = np.logical_and.accumulate(below[::-1])[::-1]   # below from here to the end
            for kind, mask in (('first', below), ('sustained', sustained)):
                row = dict(model=model, limit=lim, crossing=kind, votes_pct=np.nan,
                             stations_pct=np.nan, clock_time=pd.NaT, error_pp=np.nan)
                if mask.any():
                    r = g.loc[int(np.argmax(mask))]
                    n = int(round(r.stations_pct * E['n']))
                    row.update(votes_pct=r.votes_pct, stations_pct=r.stations_pct,
                                 clock_time=pd.Timestamp(E['times'][clock][n - 1]), error_pp=r.error_pp)
                rows.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

3.5 Findings

  • Shape of the error (Figure 7): it falls with little oscillation, from 2–3 pp at 0.1% until it crosses zero between 10% and 15% of the votes. It then turns slightly negative, bottoming out at −0.10 to −0.12 pp between 20% and 30% (subsection 3.2.2), before shrinking again.
  • The 0.25 pp target: boosting reaches it at 4.85% of the votes (17:25 on October 30), Ridge and Bayesian at 5.77%, and the swings at 6.33%. Boosting crosses with an error of 0.230 pp, just under the target; with some margin, I would use about 6%.
  • Wider limits: 1 pp at 0.8% to 1.3% of the votes; 0.5 pp at 3.1% to 4.4%.
  • The 0.1 pp limit: the first crossing comes at 6.9% to 9.9%, but it does not hold because of the trough. The sustained crossing requires 31.9% (Ridge and Bayesian) or 38.2% (boosting and swings).
  • Strategy: I chose boosting, by less than one percentage point of votes counted over Ridge and Bayesian, in a single election. The five models follow similar curves: what drives the error is the selection bias of the first stations, not the model.
  • Bayesian: coverage of 0% to 7%, when it should be 95%. The half-width would need to be multiplied by about 6 to 11. I therefore use the empirical share from 2022 as the stopping rule.
  • Caveat: these shares apply to the 2022 arrival order. In section 5.3, I check whether they hold in 2026.

4. Simulating the 2026 count

I apply the strategy from section 3 (STRATEGY) as if the count were under way: at each checkpoint, the model says who wins and by what share. I do not compute the error here, because the final result does not exist in real time; I leave it for section 5.3.

4.1 How it works

  • Checkpoints: from 0.5% to 5% of the votes, in steps of 0.5, plus 10% and 20% for comparison.
  • Inputs: as in 2022, the runoff votes of the stations already counted and the first round of the rest. The final result does not enter the model.
  • % counted and % estimated: the position of each checkpoint depends on the official vote total, known only at the end (% counted). % estimated recalculates that share using only what is known during the count.
  • 2022 error: the largest absolute error of the same model in 2022 from that share of votes onward. It is a reference, not a guaranteed bound.

    for checkpoint, n in batches(E, clock, checkpoints):
        ctx = context(E, E['order'][clock], n)
        est = model(E, ctx)['estimate']
        total = ctx['V'] + ctx['wu'].sum()                     # total valid votes expected
        rows.append(dict(checkpoint=checkpoint, stations=n, clock_time=pd.Timestamp(E['times'][clock][n - 1]),
                           votes_counted=ctx['V'], votes_13=ctx['A'], votes_22=ctx['V'] - ctx['A'],
                           pct_counted=100 * ctx['V'] / E['v2'].sum(), pct_estimated=100 * ctx['V'] / total,
                           partial_13=100 * ctx['A'] / ctx['V'], proj_13=100 * est, proj_22=100 * (1 - est),
                           votes_13_proj=est * total, votes_22_proj=(1 - est) * total))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

5.1 The series for 13 and 22

Cumulative series at each tabulated timestamp. The lead is the total votes for 13 minus those for 22. Its sign changes define three intervals: - before: until 22 pulls ahead; - during: from the first to the last batch with 22 ahead, including brief oscillations; - after: from the batch in which 13 retakes the lead for good until the end.

I start the search for the first time 22 pulls ahead at 1% of the votes, because the lead in the earliest batches flips by noise.

Computes the swing gap between remaining and counted stations at any point in the count.

def gap_swing(E):
    o = E['order']['tabulated']
    a1, v1, a2, v2 = (np.cumsum(E[k][o]) for k in ('a1', 'v1', 'a2', 'v2'))
    counted = 100 * (a2 / v2 - a1 / v1)
    with np.errstate(divide='ignore', invalid='ignore'):
        remaining = 100 * ((a2[-1] - a2) / (v2[-1] - v2) - (a1[-1] - a1) / (v1[-1] - v1))
    pct_v = 100 * v2 / v2[-1]
    k = np.flatnonzero((pct_v >= .5) & (pct_v <= 50))[::150]
    return pct_v[k], (remaining - counted)[k]

  • Running tally: between 2% and 3% it is closer to the result (error of 0.02 to 0.43 pp), but then it drifts away: −0.62 at 5%, −1.29 at 10% and −2.20 at 20%. The model is better from 3.5% onward.
  • Other models: the 2022 ranking is reversed. Boosting has the largest error of the five from 1.5% onward; national swing, the simplest, is the best from 2.5% onward. At 5%: national swing 0.35, logit 0.39, Ridge 0.44, Bayesian 0.47 and boosting 0.48 pp. None meets the target after 1%.
  • Cause: at 5%, the counted stations have a swing of +6.35 pp and the remaining ones +6.64; the approximate calculation gives −0.27 pp, close to the −0.35 of national swing. In 2022 this gap closed near 10% of the votes; in 2026 it reaches 0.60 pp at 30% and stays there at 50%.

6. Conclusion

It is possible to make the prediction with only 2% of the votes counted while keeping the error below 0.5 pp. The country’s heterogeneity, combined with the sequence of the count, causes the prediction error to oscillate even as the sample grows. If I had used a random sample, the error would probably tend to fall, but I chose to reproduce the real conditions of the count.

I could apply other models and refinements to try to keep the absolute error as small as possible with the smallest possible sample, without breaking the rule of following the actual sequence in which votes are tallied.

I have no political purpose in this study. My goal is to understand possible approaches to predicting election results.

In general, to build a prediction model for this setting, I have to rely on the profiles of the polling stations and on the change each station showed between the first and second rounds to estimate the overall behavior of all stations and, therefore, the result of the election.
